# Brand Brief

Every stage reads one brief. Keep it in the working folder as `brand-brief.md`.
When the offer, proof or price changes, update the brief, not the prompts.

If there is no brief, draft one from the website, product docs and anything
the person has shared, mark every unknown `[ASK]`, and ask for those before
writing copy. Never fill a gap with an invented number, review or result.

## Template

```md
# Brand Brief

1. What we sell, in one sentence:
2. Price and current offer:
3. Who buys (role, situation, where they are):
4. The moment right before they buy (what just happened?):
5. What they tried before, and why it fell short:
6. The result they get, and how fast:
7. Proof we can show (reviews, numbers, awards, press, guarantees), each with its source:
8. Competitors to watch (names and Facebook Pages):
9. Customer words (10 real quotes from reviews, support, sales calls, comments):
10. Claims we must never make:
11. Conversion we optimise for (purchase, install, signup, lead) and where it is counted:
12. Target cost per conversion and/or target ROAS:
13. Monthly ad budget and daily test budget:
14. Markets and languages:
15. Brand voice in three words:
16. Words, styles or visuals we never use:
```

## Rules Every Stage Follows

- Read the brief first. Never contradict it.
- Use only proof listed in item 7. Where an idea needs proof that isn't there,
  write `[PROOF NEEDED: what]` and keep going; it can't launch until filled.
- Check every claim against item 10 before it reaches an ad.
- Mark anything that spends money, launches, pauses or changes a budget
  `NEEDS APPROVAL`, and wait for the person's yes to that specific action.
- Show the maths behind every number.
- Name ads consistently so results can be grouped later. With a `matrix`
  render job, creative ids already carry round, hook, layout and CTA; keep
  them. Otherwise use `MMDD_Angle_Format_H#_v#` (for example
  `1110_BackOnTrack_UGC_H3_v1`).
- Take competitors' angles, never their words, images or brand.
- Plain language, short sentences, no hype words.

## Customer Language

Hooks and copy come from customer words, not the model's imagination. If item
9 is thin, ask for exports of reviews, support tickets, sales-call notes or ad
comments before writing hooks. Thirty real quotes beat any prompt.
